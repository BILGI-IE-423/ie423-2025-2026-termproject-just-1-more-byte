If personality shapes how we speak, can classical NLP recover those patterns from text alone?

This project treats written language as a measurable signal — not a mirror of the self, but a window into statistical tendencies in expression.

Chapter 01 · Context

Why personality and language?

The Myers–Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) describes personality along four binary dimensions — how people orient energy (I/E), process information (N/S), decide (T/F), and structure life (J/P).

MBTI is widely used but scientifically debated. We do not treat it as ground truth psychology; we treat it as a structured label space for studying whether language carries detectable personality-related signals.

Why this is an NLP problem

Personality may influence word choice, emotional tone, abstraction, and social framing — all observable in text. Machine learning lets us test which dimensions leave measurable linguistic traces.

Chapter 02 · Research Questions

What we set out to answer

Three questions guide the pipeline — each mapped to a specific stage of analysis.

RQ1 · Predictability

Which MBTI dimensions are most predictable from text?

Not all personality axes may be equally visible in language. We rank dimensions by macro F1 to see where text carries the strongest signal.

Answered by: modeling → evaluation → results ranking

RQ2 · Linguistic patterns

Which words and phrases associate with each dimension?

Beyond accuracy, we ask why models succeed — which vocabulary aligns with Thinking vs Feeling, Introversion vs Extraversion, and so on.

Answered by: logistic regression coefficients → interpretability analysis

RQ3 · Style vs topic

Can dimensions still be predicted after removing topical vocabulary?

If personality survives without content words, the signal lives in how people write — not just what they discuss.

Planned: style-only features (future work)

Chapter 03 · Methodological choice

Why predict dimensions, not 16 types?

Instead of classifying all 16 MBTI types directly, we decompose personality into four independent binary tasks:

  • I/E Introversion vs Extraversion
  • N/S Intuition vs Sensing
  • T/F Thinking vs Feeling
  • J/P Judging vs Perceiving

Why this matters

  • Less imbalance — binary splits are more balanced than rare 16-type classes
  • More interpretable — coefficients map to single psychological axes
  • More stable — smaller label space reduces spurious type-level patterns
  • Psychologically meaningful — dimensions are the unit MBTI theory actually proposes
Chapter 04 · Dataset

The corpus we studied

We use the public Kaggle MBTI Personality Type dataset — forum posts from users who self-reported their four-letter type.

Each row is one person: their declared MBTI type and aggregated posts. Labels are self-reported, not clinically validated — a deliberate trade-off that gives scale but introduces noise.

Dataset realities
  • Self-report bias — users choose their own type; labels may reflect identity performance
  • Internet language — forum slang, memes, and community jargon add noise
  • Imperfect validity — MBTI is popular but contested in academic psychology

Acknowledging these limits makes our findings more credible, not weaker.

Clean corpus

Each user's posts are concatenated and cleaned: URLs removed, explicit MBTI type mentions stripped (to prevent label leakage), special characters normalized, text lowercased.

We deliberately chose moderate preprocessing. Aggressive normalization — stemming everything, removing all punctuation, stripping emotional markers — could destroy the very stylistic signals personality might leave behind.

Key decision

Preprocessing removes noise and leakage, but preserves vocabulary richness, emotional words, and informal expression — because those may be the personality signal.

Chapter 06 · Feature engineering

From words to numbers

TF-IDF vectorization

Text becomes a sparse matrix of 5,000 term weights. TF-IDF highlights words distinctive to a document relative to the corpus — effective for forum text where topic and style intertwine.

Stopword removal

Standard English stopwords removed to reduce noise, while keeping emotionally and stylistically loaded terms that may carry personality signal.

Stratified split

80/20 train–test split stratified on full MBTI type — ensuring rare types appear in both sets and evaluation reflects real imbalance.

Why TF-IDF, not transformers?

This project prioritizes interpretability and linguistic analysis. TF-IDF + linear models yield inspectable coefficients — you can read which words drive predictions. Deep models may score higher but obscure the language–personality link we set out to study.

Chapter 07 · Modeling

Three interpretable classifiers

Each model trains independently on all four dimensions — four binary classification tasks × three algorithms.

Logistic Regression

Linear decision boundary in TF-IDF space. Coefficients directly reveal predictive terms. Strong baseline for sparse text — and our best performer overall.

Linear SVM

Maximum-margin linear classifier. Handles high-dimensional sparse features well; coefficients analogous to LR for interpretability.

Random Forest

Non-linear ensemble baseline. Tests whether tree interactions capture signal linear models miss — at the cost of harder interpretation.

Handling imbalance

All models use class_weight="balanced" — automatically up-weighting minority classes so the classifier cannot ignore rare traits.

Why linear models fit TF-IDF

Sparse bag-of-words vectors live in high-dimensional space where linear separators often work well. With thousands of features and limited samples per class, simpler models generalize more reliably than complex ones.

Chapter 08 · Evaluation strategy

Measuring fairly under imbalance

Macro F1 (primary metric)

Averages F1 across both classes equally — so a model cannot look good by always predicting the majority trait. Essential when classes are uneven.

Accuracy

Reported but not trusted alone. A model predicting only "Introvert" could score high accuracy while failing completely on Extraverts.

ROC-AUC

Measures ranking quality across thresholds — how well the model separates classes probabilistically.

Confusion matrices

Show where errors concentrate — which trait pairs are confused, revealing asymmetric predictability.

Why might T/F be most predictable?

Thinking and Feeling orientations may produce the most distinct vocabulary — analytical vs emotional language, systems-talk vs relationship-talk. Our interpretability analysis (Chapter 10) confirms terms like logic, feel, and people dominate the T/F boundary.

Chapter 10 · Linguistic interpretability

Hidden traces in vocabulary

RQ2 — Which linguistic patterns associate with each dimension?

Thinking language leaned toward logic, systems, analysis, and detached framing — words that organize and evaluate.

Feeling language emphasized emotion, relationships, values, and personal experience — words that connect and express.

Future work · RQ3

Beyond topical vocabulary

The hypothesis

Remove content words (nouns, proper nouns, domain terms) and retain function words, punctuation, sentence length. If models still predict personality, the signal is stylistic — not just topical.

Planned pipeline

POS-tag with NLTK, filter content words, build style-only features, re-train Logistic Regression, compare macro F1 against the TF-IDF baseline.

Current status

Stub implemented in 05_build_style_features.py. Reliable POS-tagging on noisy forum text needs further tuning — documented as the logical next step.

Other directions: transformer embeddings, multilingual corpora, deeper stylometric features, and ethical auditing of deployment contexts.

Chapter 11 · Limitations & ethics

What this project does not claim

MBTI validity

MBTI lacks strong scientific consensus. We study linguistic correlates of self-reported types — not verified personality structure.

Self-report bias

Users choose their own label. Online personas may exaggerate or perform type-consistent language consciously or unconsciously.

Topical leakage

TF-IDF captures topic as well as style. Community-specific jargon may inflate scores without revealing deep personality traits (RQ3 addresses this).

Personality ≠ destiny

Statistical patterns in text must never be used to judge, hire, or categorize individuals. Language reveals tendencies — not identity.

Chapter 12 · Conclusion

Patterns, not identities

Language does not perfectly reveal who we are.
But it leaves measurable psychological traces.

We showed that classical NLP can detect some MBTI dimensions from text — especially Thinking vs Feeling — and that interpretable models expose the vocabulary behind those predictions. The signal is real but partial, noisy, and ethically bounded.

Personality is more than words. Our models capture statistical tendencies in written expression — invitations to further study, not definitions of the self.
